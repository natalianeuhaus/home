01 / STATEMENT

The Milk Route traces radioactive fallout from nuclear tests in Nevada as it moved into the Dairy Belt, passing through milk, bodies, and generations, leaving clusters of thyroid cancer and long-term illness behind.

But the project also looks beyond the human record, toward the land that absorbed the fallout—the desert, water, soil, plants, animals, roads, and abandoned places that continue to carry its residue.

Between 1951 and 1962, the U.S. Atomic Energy Commission oversaw 100 atmospheric tests in the Nevada desert. Together, they produced about one megaton of explosive force—roughly equivalent to 67 Hiroshima bombs or 48 Nagasaki bombs, and about 28 times the two bombings combined. The fallout did not remain in Nevada; it drifted across the continental United States, settling onto pasture and entering the food chain through milk.

03 / THE ROUTE

A contamination pathway disguised as an ordinary food chain.

The project follows movement rather than a single site: from the Nevada desert, into pasture and cattle, through milk, into bodies—and outward again into memory, illness, and landscape.
