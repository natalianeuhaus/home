01 / FILM

The Milk Route traces radioactive fallout from nuclear tests in Nevada as it moved into the Dairy Belt, passing through milk, bodies, and generations, leaving clusters of thyroid cancer and long-term illness behind.

But the project also looks beyond the human record, toward the land that absorbed the fallout—the desert, water, soil, plants, animals, roads, and abandoned places that continue to carry its residue.

02 / THE ROUTE

A contamination pathway disguised as an ordinary food chain.

The project follows movement rather than a single site: from the Nevada desert, into pasture and cattle, through milk, into bodies—and outward again into memory, illness, and landscape.

04 / EVIDENCE

The archive accumulates rather than explains.

Forecasts, test-site reports, milk tables, monitoring records, and animal experiments sit together as fragments of the same system. Read separately they are bureaucratic. Seen together, the route becomes visible.

INVISIBLE / INGESTED / INHERITED
05 / RESIDUE

The story does not end when the fallout leaves the air.

It settles. It moves. It is consumed. It enters systems designed to look ordinary: farms, roads, kitchens, schools, families.

The final chapter stays with the places that remain after the event—the land, water, plants, animals, architecture, and abandoned structures that hold the history without speaking it.
