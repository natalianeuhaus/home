Greetings from Niagara

The investigation

The unfortunate demise of Niagara Falls is rooted in the very force that drew industry to it: the waterfalls. Abundant, inexpensive hydroelectric power accelerated industrial growth beginning in the late nineteenth century, bringing severe pollution in its wake.

Beginning in 1942, during the Manhattan Project and continuing through the Cold War, Niagara Falls became a major center for the experimentation, research, development, and production of atomic bomb materials. U.S. Department of Energy records identify local industry during this period as the nation’s largest production center for uranium metal from ore. The nuclear buildup left a radioactive legacy across the region, one whose consequences extend far beyond the factories where these materials were handled.

A 2022 study by Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center, WNY Cancer Snapshot, found an increased incidence of multiple cancers in Western New York compared with state and national averages.

For decades, government studies have documented radioactive contamination across the region. Yet the relationship between that contamination and the persistently high burden of cancer and endocrine disease remains largely absent from public understanding. Greetings from Niagara follows that evidence across archives, industrial sites, neighborhoods, roads, waterways, and the lives of residents who continue to inhabit this landscape.
