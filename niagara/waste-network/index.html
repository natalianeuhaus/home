Production, storage, and disposal

The radioactive slag could have come from any of these sites.

This map brings together documented uranium-production sites and additional Niagara factories named or tested in later waste investigations. The surviving record cannot identify one source for every later finding.

Solid waste

Slag and contaminated fill moved outward.

Radioactive material was later found beneath roads, driveways, parking lots, and properties across parts of Niagara and Erie Counties—far beyond the facilities that generated, handled, stored, or received it.

Liquid waste

Other wastes moved through water and underground.

Records describe liquid and process wastes entering disposal wells, sewers, creeks, and rivers—pathways that could carry contamination beyond the place where it was produced.

Historical movement

Radioactive slag was excavated, transported, and reused as ordinary fill.

Records describe slag leaving industrial and federal sites by truck. Some was sold, taken home, buried, or used beneath driveways, roads, and other construction.

November 4, 2017

New York required radiation screening at covered landfills.

Landfills accepting municipal solid waste or drilling and production waste—public or private—must monitor incoming loads with fixed radiation detectors.

The present-day gap

Reused fill may never pass through a radiation detector.

Covered landfills must screen incoming loads. But excavated material, slag, and reused fill can move directly to another property or project—and New York’s routine fill analysis does not list radionuclides.
