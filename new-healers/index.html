Healers

Sex Work as a Calling

“I live to connect, enjoy, and create. Spend a little time with me, I’ll show you what I mean. Artist, designer, reiki master.”

01Goldie

“Healers: Sex Work as a Calling” gives the viewer an intimate new perspective into the lives of those who chose this profession, humanizing what has been stigmatized and ostracized for centuries. Sex work is not only work; for many, it can offer healing from sexual trauma or gender dysphoria, or provide something so innate to humankind yet so taboo: sexual pleasure.

This body of work is an homage to everyone in this profession: to those who fought and keep on fighting for decriminalization, and to those who constantly fear being arrested, deported, or having their children taken away from them. This constant fear and exposure to violence is why Human Rights Watch supports the full decriminalization of sex work. Since 2021, New York City became the first city in the United States to no longer prosecute sex work; hopefully, more will follow.

Before the photographs

How Healers began

Healers began with friendship. Getting to know these extraordinary women made me confront my own prejudices and unlearn the deeply ingrained assumption that all sex work is synonymous with trafficking or coercion. Through them, I came to understand that sex work can also be a choice and to see it through a sex-positive lens—one that recognizes consensual sex work as labor and respects the agency of those who choose it.
0208

The introduction

Reaching out

For a year, I reviewed online profiles and reached out to women I thought might be interested—usually twenty to thirty each month, from a range of ages, racial and cultural backgrounds, and areas of expertise within sex work. I wanted to portray the fullness of their lives rather than reduce them to stereotypes about sex work. Only one or two would respond. Understandably, most were cautious about allowing a stranger with a camera into their homes and lives.

The introduction

Original outreach March 22, 2022 · 1:22 PM

Female Queer Photographer documenting sex-workers life

Hi! My name is Natalia Neuhaus and I’m a queer photographer who works on long-term photo projects. My latest long-term project is about burlesque. It’s not so much about the show but more about this queer and diverse community that through burlesque highlights that sensuality comes from within and it’s not defined or exclusive to a specific type of body or gender.

Many worlds overlap in burlesque and sex work is one of them. The most emphatic humans I’ve met are sex workers and here they are ostracised by society because the word sex is involved. I have a firm belief that many of you are healers and fulfill more than sexual fantasies just by being present and by listening. Unlike other photographers, I’m truly not interested in the sex part, I’m focusing on the mundane like your downtime or doing laundry, your plants, your home. Or those quiet moments after work.

The editorial pitch

After having no luck getting the story published in the United States, I turned to editors in Europe, hoping the work might find a more receptive audience. I included some self-promotion, mentioning the mentorship and institutional support behind the project, only in the hope that it might help open a door for the work. Rejections

That distinction is central to the project’s mission: to educate, challenge misconceptions, and offer a more nuanced understanding of sex work and those who choose it.

What followed

The work continued.

Healers was featured in LFI — Leica Fotografie International and exhibited in the United States as part of the Leica Women Foto Project group exhibition If This Isn’t Love. The work continues, with the hope that one day it will live on as a photobook.
